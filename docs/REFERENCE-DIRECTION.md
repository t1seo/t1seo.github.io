# Jieun's lakeside studio — reference direction

Research date: 2026-09-29. This document supports `REDESIGN-3D.md`; its layout contract remains authoritative. The intended result is an inhabited, desirable real-time 3D studio with a deep view beyond the window, seasonal possessions, and the existing interactive ID badge.

Research used the installed `design-toolkit` skill, web/image search, and the Mobbin connector. The references below are individual pages/screens, not platform homepages. They inform original composition and interaction decisions; no reference scene image is a runtime background or a copied asset.

## Five decisions to implement

1. **Put the viewer inside the room.** Use the agreed three-quarter camera, with the chair/rug in the foreground, desk and lamp in the middle, and trees/lake/mountains beyond the thick window frame. A packed left bookcase balances the large opening. Preserve an uninterrupted central lake view rather than lining all trees up in front of it. The room should fill the viewport: avoid an isolated miniature surrounded by empty canvas. Sources: [winter office](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/20-warm-and-cozy-winter-home-office-designs--557742735119531899/) and [cabin library](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/furkan-on-instagram-cabin-life-credit-cozywonderspots-these-imaginary-scenes-were-created-with-the-help-of-various-programs-cozyliving--608830443401907656/).

2. **Make “full of possessions” read as a life, with clusters and breathing room.** Aim for approximately 70–85% occupied shelf width as an art-direction target, varying upright books, short horizontal stacks, ceramics, a framed photograph, a clock, and a storage box. Combine three or five related objects in a cluster, vary height, and leave part of the desktop usable. Add a throw draped over the chair and a basket near the floor. The silhouette and object scale matter more than adding hundreds of uniformly repeated small items. Source: [cabin library](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/furkan-on-instagram-cabin-life-credit-cozywonderspots-these-imaginary-scenes-were-created-with-the-help-of-various-programs-cozyliving--608830443401907656/).

3. **Separate materials and light sources.** Use walnut/oak with visible edge highlights, a cream woven rug and linen curtains, dark powder-coated monitor hardware, small brass details, and glazed ceramics. Keep the outdoor fill cool and the practical lamps amber. Lit pools should have a source and fall away into quieter corners; the entire room should not receive a flat orange wash. Desk legs, shelf junctions, the chair, and objects sitting on surfaces need visible grounding shadows. Sources: [winter office](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/20-warm-and-cozy-winter-home-office-designs--557742735119531899/) and [Christmas office](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/cozy-modern-farmhouse-home-office-with-festive-christmas-charm--213076626116078021/).

4. **Season changes must alter the room's silhouette.** Winter adds a full dimensional tree, draped garlands and small fairy lights, wreath, presents of different sizes, cocoa, and a cream/red wool throw. Spring adds a flowering branch and fresh light textiles; summer replaces the hot drink with an iced glass, exposes lighter linen, and adds a fan; autumn adds an amber branch, pumpkins, stacked reading material, and a thicker rust throw. Keep enough permanent books/objects to make this the same lived-in room. Source for the whole-room winter transformation: [Christmas office](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/cozy-modern-farmhouse-home-office-with-festive-christmas-charm--213076626116078021/). The other seasonal arrangements are original direction for this project.

5. **Let the room dominate and the UI stay at the edges.** Small identity/navigation at the top, a compact bottom time/season control, and short object-specific hover/focus labels. A large scene does not need large marketing text over its focal point. Preserve the supplied badge's typography, material appearance, drag and flip behavior; locate it at a believable wall hook and retain a readable view. Reference the grouped action shape of [21st Minimal Dock](https://21st.dev/@jatin-yadav05/components/minimal-dock) and the environmental hierarchy of [Pillow](https://mobbin.com/screens/1b66503c-8944-4928-b2a5-3d59c440d4f1) / [Oura](https://mobbin.com/screens/3b2948a4-734e-4c08-a403-2c6ae0d5d1f7), while keeping visible labels and keyboard access. These are layout references; no React component dependency is required.

## Review the actual render against these targets

- At normal desktop size, a visitor should immediately see a room, a view, and a working desk; no cropped giant prop or badge should hide the central composition.
- At least three visibly different depth layers should be present both indoors and outdoors. Outdoor trees should have trunks/branches and varied shapes; the lake should occupy actual ground space beyond the aperture.
- Book bindings, curved lamp geometry, curtain folds, chair upholstery, desk bevels, window recesses, and rug texture should still be discernible at the displayed scale. Small detail is useful only when the lighting lets it read.
- Compare summer noon with winter night. Both should be legible, but their interior silhouettes, fabrics, practical lighting, foliage, and weather should differ clearly.
- Winter should communicate Christmas before the visitor reads a season label. Test that gifts, ornaments, and garland form a scene rather than an isolated tree beside unchanged furniture.
- Click and keyboard activation should create a visible local response: light reaches surfaces, curtains change the aperture, steam rises from the cup, and monitor content actually changes. Keep the retained badge fully usable.
- Inspect mobile separately. Its narrower view may need camera adaptation and relocated controls, but must preserve the room's depth and a useful target size.

These are design review criteria, not evidence that implementation already meets them. Root's browser inspection and iteration loop determines acceptance.
